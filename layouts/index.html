Wavey is an open-source WiFi CSI sensing system that turns ordinary WiFi radios into a privacy-preserving sensor. By reading Channel State Information (CSI) from low-cost ESP32 sensing nodes, Wavey detects occupancy, motion, and presence in a space - with no cameras, no wearables, and no app on the people being sensed.

How it works · Use cases · Get started · GitHub · Console · Discord

What is WiFi CSI sensing?

Channel State Information (CSI) describes how a WiFi signal propagates from a transmitter to a receiver - its amplitude, phase, and delay across many OFDM subcarriers. Unlike a single RSSI value, CSI is fine-grained and highly sensitive to the environment. When a person walks, gestures, or even breathes, their body perturbs these radio paths in consistent, measurable ways. Wavey captures those perturbations and converts them into spatial and motion information - a technique known as device-free, passive WiFi sensing.

What Wavey can do

  • Device-free occupancy detection (is anyone in the room?)
  • Motion and activity inference
  • Presence and micro-motion sensing (including stillness and breathing)
  • Real-time RF signal visualization
  • Spatial occupancy estimation
  • Multi-node mesh sensing
  • RF fingerprinting and scene-change detection
  • Real-time CSI data collection and processing
  • ESP32-CSI based sensing nodes
  • Python-based analysis pipeline

Use cases

  • Device-free occupancy detection
  • Motion & activity detection
  • Presence & breathing detection
  • Elder-care & fall-aware monitoring
  • Security & intrusion detection
  • Smart-home automation
  • Occupancy analytics

Why Wavey

  • Privacy-first. No cameras and no images - Wavey senses the radio environment, not your appearance. See WiFi sensing vs cameras →
  • Device-free. The people being detected carry nothing - no phone, tag, or wearable required.
  • Low-cost hardware. Built on commodity ESP32 chips instead of expensive radar or LiDAR.
  • Open source. Inspect, self-host, and extend it. View on GitHub →

Built for developers and self-hosters

Wavey targets the open-source ESP32-CSI niche: real, hackable WiFi sensing you can run yourself. Sensing nodes stream CSI to a real-time pipeline, and you can explore live signals in the Wavey Console. The approach builds on a growing body of WiFi-sensing research, from academic work like CMU's DensePose-from-WiFi to the IEEE 802.11bf WiFi-sensing standard.
